1a Default view · whole UK
Same shell as Assess area and Find gaps: rail selects the mode, left panel holds controls, right panel holds results. Paperwork is collapsed into developments, so one site is one pin and one row.
1
Sixth rail icon enters the mode; active state is the filled dark circle used by the other modes.
2
Scope toggle. Whole UK and My patch share one filter model — switching re-scopes, never navigates. My patch is disabled until a patch exists.
3
Filters live in the left panel as summary rows, mirroring Assess area's controls. Each row opens its own control; "All filters" opens the full panel (1b). Active filters also read back as removable chips over the map.
4
Results header follows Find gaps: big count, mono sublabel, sort pills, CSV export.
5
Row ↔ pin linkage. Hovering a row enlarges its pin, adds a ring and names it; hovering a pin tints the row and scrolls it into view. Clicking a row flies the map to the pin and opens its card (1c).
Grouping rule. Documents group into one development when they share a site boundary or parent reference. The "N documents" chip is the same number in the row and on the pin tooltip.

1b All filters · one set, three hosts
The panel widens over the map rather than opening a modal, so the map stays readable while filtering. The same component serves the whole-UK view, the patch view and the patch's saved criteria (1e) — whatever is set here is what the weekly summary uses.
1
Time frame is single-select. Custom opens a two-month range picker inline.
2
Dwelling minimum is a number input with steppers, clamped at 15. Typing lower snaps back and shows the helper line.
3
Residential and commercial are independent toggles — either, both, or neither. Neither on means no use filter, not no results. Commercial borrows Find gaps' teal so the two use types stay separable on the map.
4
Brand proximity picks any tracked brand with a radius — "show me everything within 2 miles of an Aldi", not only the user's own estate.
5
Match count recomputes live (debounced) in the mono footer. Apply collapses the panel; Cancel reverts to the last applied set.

1c Pin clicked · development detail
One card answers every question a surveyor asks about a site, then lists the paperwork underneath it.
1
Card is anchored beside its pin, never over it, and flips side when the pin sits near an edge. Status pill, address, use type and authority sit above the fold.
2
Fixed fact grid: dwellings (residential only), planning reference in mono, decision date, validation date. Brand distance shows only when a proximity filter is on.
3
Every document links out to that reference on the authority's own portal; the primary button opens the lead application. Named per authority so the user knows where they're going.
4
The sidebar row expands in step with the card — selection is one state shared by both, so there's never a card open without its row marked.
5
Site boundary draws where the authority publishes one, so a 320-home scheme reads as an area rather than a dot.

1d Free draw · the only option
Drawing takes over the whole view: the results panel closes, the other rail modes grey out, and Export is disabled until the patch is saved or cancelled. Nothing else competes for the click.
1
Three-step tracker in the left panel, reusing Assess area's numbered list pattern. Point count updates as they click.
2
Live area and match estimate so an oversized patch is obvious before saving. Undo removes the last point; Start again clears the shape.
3
Mode toast states the keyboard exits. Esc cancels the whole draw and returns to 1a.
4
Crosshair cursor with a rubber-band edge to the next point; the first vertex enlarges as a snap target when the cursor is near it.
Self-intersection is the one error state: the offending edge turns amber and closing is blocked until the point is moved.

1g Summary slides over the map
Generated every Monday from the previous 7 days inside the patch, using the patch's saved filters. It is a panel, not a page — the map stays behind it so a pin is one click away.
1
Headline is the count, not a greeting. Dismiss with ✕, Esc, or a click on the map; reopen from the panel card in 1f.
2
Week stepper. ‹ walks back through archived weeks (1h); › is disabled on the latest week and labelled as such.
3
Three counters answer "is this week worth my time": residential with total dwellings, commercial, and how many sit near a watched brand.
4
AI summary sits above the list, written from the same week's applications — what moved, where, and which one to look at first. It names the developments it draws on so the claims are checkable against the rows below, and it appears in the email too (1i). On a zero-application week it is replaced by the plain "nothing new" line rather than padded prose.
5
Grouped by use, newest first, each row carrying dwellings, status, date, authority and document count. Clicking a row closes the panel and opens that development's card on the map.
6
Email toggle repeats here — the moment they see the value is the moment to offer it. Export week gives the same content as CSV.
Quiet week. Zero new applications still generates a summary: "Nothing new in West Yorkshire this week", with the week stepper and email toggle intact — silence should be a result, not a missing screen.

1h Clicking back through weeks
The date label is itself the picker, so stepping one week and jumping five use the same control. Weeks with nothing new are listed with a zero rather than hidden — the record of a quiet week is information too.
Opening an archived week also re-scopes the map behind the panel to that week, and an amber-free info strip states it so nobody reads old data as current.
Retention. Weeks are kept from the patch's creation date; the header states how many exist. Editing the patch boundary or filters does not rewrite past weeks — they stay as they were sent.

1i The opted-in email
Same three counters and the same grouping as the in-app panel, capped at the top few rows so it stays scannable on a phone. One button back into the patch.
Opt-in lives in three places and is one state: the save step (1e), the summary footer (1g), and the patch menu (1j). Turning it off anywhere keeps the in-app summary generating.
Quiet weeks aren't sent. If nothing matched, no email goes out — the in-app week still exists and says so. Unsubscribe is in every send.

1j Patch menu · editing the boundary
One ⋯ menu on both the map chip and the panel header. Editing the boundary keeps the existing shape and exposes draggable vertices — a midpoint handle appears between two vertices to add a point, and dragging a vertex off-shape deletes it.
Save recomputes the match count immediately; the next summary uses the new shape, while past weeks stay untouched.
Delete West Yorkshire?
You'll lose the boundary, its filters and 14 weekly summaries. The weekly email stops. Applications themselves are unaffected — you can still browse the whole UK.
Only want a different area? Edit the boundary instead and keep your history.
Keep patch Delete patch
1j Delete confirmation
The dialog names what is actually lost — boundary, filters and the summary history — and offers the lighter action, editing the boundary, rather than only Cancel. No typed confirmation: a patch is redrawable, and the count of summaries is the real warning.
After deleting, the mode falls back to the whole-UK view with the Create your patch card restored (1a), and a toast offers a single undo.
